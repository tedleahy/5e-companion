5e Companion

Design system, v0.4

Distilled from character-list.html and character-sheet.html. It records the "riso" direction: two-ink risograph printing on toned paper, with a third overprint colour where the inks would overlap. Everything on this page is rendered with the same CSS as the mockups, so the specimens are the source of truth, not the prose.

Principles

What the character list and character sheet mockups are doing, stated so later screens can do the same thing.

Two inks, one paper
  • Structural blue does the work: plates, borders, primary numbers, text.
  • Accent rose is the second pass: offset shadows, the hurt state, and the primary call to action on each screen or sheet.
  • Purple is the overprint. Use it sparingly as a third token colour and for concentration-type marks.
Print, not glass
  • Hard offset shadows in flat accent ink. No blur, no gradients on components.
  • Square corners on rectangles. Circles for tokens and the armour class ring.
  • The paper is textured with a dot and line grid so cards sit on something.
Built for play
  • Status is readable at a glance: filled meter, a mark or two. Token colour is identity, not status.
  • Below half hit points changes the meter colour. The player should not need to read the number.
  • Dense is fine, unscannable is not. Every row has the same shape.
Avoid
  • Rounded corners on cards, buttons or chips.
  • Soft drop shadows, glows, glassmorphism.
  • Introducing green, amber or red for status. Hurt is rose, fine is the token colour.
  • Adding a sixth colour. Derive it from the five inks instead.
  • Raw hex values, or opacity on text, in component styles. Use a token, so a new ink set, including a future dark one, needs no component changes.

Derived tokens

Formulas are in the mockup's derive() function. Text colours come from mixing structural ink toward near-black, secondary text mixes ink toward paper, and every translucent tint is an alpha of one of the inks. Every token that carries text is contrast-targeted. The derivation steps through mix ratios and keeps the first one that reaches 4.5:1 on every surface the text sits on, so the mixer cannot produce a failing palette whatever inks are chosen.

Paper surface

The page background is paper with a printed grid: structural dots on a 9px pitch, accent lines on a 27px pitch. It is the only decorative texture in the system, and it stays behind everything.

paper + grid 
background:
  radial-gradient(var(--dot) 1.1px, transparent 1.1px) 0 0 / 9px 9px,
  linear-gradient(var(--line) 1px, transparent 1px) 0 0 / 100% 27px,
  linear-gradient(90deg, var(--line) 1px, transparent 1px) 0 0 / 27px 100%,
  var(--paper);

Cards use plain --sheet with no texture, which is what makes them read as stuck on. The sticky footer fades from transparent to paper so the grid is visible above the button.

RuleDetail
TrackingTitles and names are tracked tight (-.02em to -.03em). Nothing else is tracked.
Weights700 for titles, names, numbers, buttons. 600 for chips and marks. 500 for the hit point line. 400 for input text and subtitles. 700 is the cap: Space Grotesk has no heavier face, and React Native will not fake one.
CaseSentence case everywhere, including buttons, column headers and the names of features, feats, subclasses, invocations, spells and items: Dark One’s own luck, Pact of the blade, Ring of spell storing. Proper nouns keep their capitals, as in Hunger of Hadar or the Dark One. Only the "AC" ring label is caps.
PersonSecond person for the player and third person for the character: “until you confirm”, “set by you”, “when she drops a foe”. Quoted rules text stays as printed, even where it says “you”.
SpellingAmerican spelling in the app, so labels match the catalog’s names: armor class, color, gray. This document and the plan stay in British English, because players never see them.
NumbersThe current value is the bold one. Maximums and units stay in dim at regular weight.
CountsSuperscript count next to the title on phone only ("6 saved"). Desktop has the room to skip it.
OpacityNever lower the opacity of text. Hierarchy comes from size, weight and the dim token, which is contrast-checked. Opacity on top of it is not.

Interaction states

States follow the print metaphor. Pressed things sink onto the paper, selected things invert, disabled things lose their ink. Nothing fades or glows, and every state change is instant. The only motion is the sheet header shrinking as the page scrolls and the dice roll; see accessibility.

PressedAnything with an offset shadow moves by the shadow's offset and the shadow disappears. That means 5px for cards and the primary button, 3px for outlined buttons. Anything without a shadow gets a 2px inset ring in its own text colour. On phone the whole character card presses.:active

DisabledNo fill, no shadow, dim text and a 2px rule-coloured outline. Controls on the plate are hidden rather than disabled, because dim text cannot reach contrast on structural.:disabled, [aria-disabled=true]

Chips live on the plate only. The active chip is a sheet-coloured cutout with structural text. Inactive chips are a translucent tint of the plate text.

Zero hit points

At 0 hit points a character is dying, stable or dead. Each state changes what sits in the meter slot and adds a bad mark, so it reads without relying on colour. Death save counts are already stored on the character, so these states need no new data. The character sheet builds on these states with its death save tab and hit point pad.

Marks on the sheet plate

On the character sheet's header plate, a mark that opens something is a button. A condition mark opens the status sheet, and the concentration mark opens the spell behind it. The dashed Condition control is the plate's Add: dashed like every Add, in the plate's own text colour. While its sheet is open the control and the mark show the pressed ring. The compact header holds one mark in the same priority order; more than one condition reads "Poisoned +1".

React Native

The app ships as React Native on web, Android and iOS. This page stays HTML because it is the quickest way to iterate on the look, and the web build is a real target. Everything above is platform-neutral: tokens, sizes, anatomy, state rules and contrast. The CSS that renders it is not. This section maps each web technique onto its native equivalent, and records the decisions that only native forces.

Meters and pips are decorative or collapsed into one control; the adjacent text carries the value. On the character sheet: switches that swap the content below are tablist with aria-selected (sheet tabs, rest length, catalog or custom, the pad's tabs, the coin switch), single choices are radiogroup with aria-checked (ranks, alignment, token colour, ability, uses, ends, gain or spend), multi-select toggles keep aria-pressed (conditions, inspiration), and a pip row is one spinbutton with aria-valuetext such as "1 of 2 left", stepped with arrow keys, Home and End. Arrow keys move a choice with roving focus. A toggle row with a details chevron is two siblings, a label and a button, so opening details never toggles the row. Bottom sheets are dialog with aria-modal: the page behind is inert, focus cycles inside, and the opener carries aria-haspopup="dialog" and aria-expanded. Disclosures report aria-expanded. The header's vitals block is one button named by its numbers, and live regions carry the notes' save status and the roll's running total.
Hit areasEvery control answers taps across at least 44 by 44. On web the extension is an invisible ::after centred on the control, so layout and appearance do not change. Where two controls sit closer than that, their areas meet at the midpoint of the gap instead of overlapping, and 24 is the floor in that direction, the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum. Chips extend to 44 tall and keep their own width. Text fields get a real 44 minimum height instead, because an overlay would cover the caret.

Decisions

Settled from 11 September 2026 onwards. Each entry carries its date; those without one are from 11 September.

  • Contrast. Text tokens are contrast-targeted in the derivation. Dim and rose text are darker, rose surfaces carry dark text, the inactive chip tint is lighter, and text never uses opacity.
  • Interaction states. Pressed sinks, focus outlines, selected inverts and disabled loses its ink. Hover is deferred.
  • Meter colour. Meters are structural for every character and turn rose below half. The unused alias that suggested token-coloured meters is gone.
  • Token colour. Stored per character as an ink role and picked at creation. The data model and creation flow now include it.
  • Zero hit points. Dying shows death save pips, stable shows an empty meter, and dead drops the token's ink.
  • Icons. Outline only, 1.8 stroke on a 16 grid, colour from context.
  • Font weights. Capped at 700, the heaviest face Space Grotesk has. 12 September 2026.
  • Font scaling. Body text scales with the operating system setting; fixed circles cap at 1.3 times. First proposal, 12 September 2026.
  • Safe areas and press feedback. The plate paints under the status bar, the Create button clears the home indicator, and Android's ripple is off. 12 September 2026.
  • Hit areas. At least 44 by 44 for every control: an invisible extension on web, hit slop on native. Neighbours meet mid-gap, with a floor of 24. 12 September 2026.
  • Primary buttons. "Only one primary per screen" becomes at most one full-width primary per screen or sheet, with repeated cards and rows allowed one small primary each for their own action. The character sheet designs settled it. 13 September 2026.
  • Selection and semantics. Selected state lives in ARIA attributes, not a data attribute: aria-selected for tabs, aria-checked for single choices including filter chips, and aria-pressed for multi-select toggles. Arrow keys move a choice with roving focus. A list card is one link stretched over the card. Token colours are named by the theme. 14 September 2026.
  • Case and person. Names of features, feats, subclasses, invocations, spells and items are sentence case, keeping proper nouns. UI copy addresses the player as “you” and refers to the character in the third person; quoted rules text is left as printed. 14 September 2026.
  • Spelling. The app’s UI copy uses American spelling to match the catalog. Internal documents stay British. 14 September 2026.
  • Draft contrast. Dim and rose text are checked against the draft tint over paper as well as over the card colour, because a draft sits on paper and its tint is see-through. Both come out a step darker than before. 14 September 2026.
  • Sheet navigation. A persistent header, tall and compact, with the tabs attached underneath. Detail screens are full-screen pushes, never bottom sheets or in-place expansions; the static bottom sheet is the system's one overlay shape, for the sheet menu, the status picker and the in-play pads. 12 and 13 September 2026.
  • Motion. Two exceptions to instant state changes: the header shrinks continuously as the page scrolls, and the dice roll animates for about half a second, stepped between fixed poses with its shadow held still. Reduced motion shows the result at once. 13 September 2026.
  • Bottom sheets are modal. A scrim in --scrim dims the page, which is inert; focus cycles inside the sheet; Escape, Close or a tap on the scrim closes it and returns focus to the opener, which carries aria-haspopup="dialog" and aria-expanded. No drag, no slide, no grab handle. 13 and 14 September 2026.
  • Editing. Persistent facts sit behind a per-section Edit; an open block takes the draft skin (dashed border, --draft-bg, no shadow) with Cancel and Save, "Was" marks on changed values, and knock-on effects spelled out. List removals are staged until Save; every Add opens its own screen and commits from its footer. 13 September 2026.
  • Destructive button. Outlined in --b-text, no shadow, never the primary look, used for anything that removes something or ends in death. 13 September 2026.

Deferred

Deliberately left until a screen needs them.

  • Hover. Desktop needs some pointer feedback. It should stay within the print metaphor and must not move layout.
  • Empty states. Design each one with its screen: no characters, no search results, no drafts.
  • Tablet. Nothing exists between 390 and 1440 wide. Decide once two or three screens show what needs to reflow.
  • Dark mode. The token rule keeps this cheap. The derivation should handle a dark paper, but no dark pairing has been tried.
  • Level-up wizard. The Character tab has its entry and the draft card; the flow itself is its own screen set.
  • Edit conflicts. lock_version catches a change made from another device while a section is open. Nothing shows what happens then.
  • Desktop and tablet sheets. The character sheet is designed at 390 only. The desktop and tablet layouts, and how its pushed screens and bottom sheets behave there, are not drawn.
