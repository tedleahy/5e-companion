Compendium · back affordance · round two

Five ways out of a category

Every option is shown twice: on the category screen (night canvas, back to the hub) and on the class detail (parchment, back to the list). The two levels use the same device in each option, so one decision covers both. Hover a control to see its state.

A

Trail

The eyebrow already says “Compendium”. Make it the link and the extra button disappears — the label you tap is the place you land.

Trade-off Nothing is added to the layout, and the path reads top-down: Compendium → Classes → Barbarian. Quietest of the five; the underline is doing all the work of saying “tappable”.

B

Codex bar

One nav row instead of a header plus a floating chip. Bare gold chevron on the left, title stays centred, 44pt target with no visible chrome until you touch it.

Trade-off The safe one: reads instantly, matches platform muscle memory, gains back a whole row of vertical space. The chevron carries no label, so it leans on the eyebrow beside it to say where “back” goes.

C

Pennant

A claret banner hung from the header rule, its fishtail tucked behind the parchment. The card looks pinned to the page rather than floating on the dark.

Trade-off The most characterful, and the one device that stays put while the label under it changes with depth. Costs ~14px of card height and needs care so the toggle row never sits under the tail.

D

Quill mark

No pill, no fill: a drawn arrow in gold ink, an italic word, and a rule that runs off to the right. On press the stroke redraws itself, left to right.

Trade-off Handwritten where everything else is set type, and the full-width rule gives the thin stroke a real target. Needs a react-native-svg path and a Reanimated dash to survive the port; static, it is still the lightest of the five.

E

Spine

The riskiest: back moves out of the vertical flow entirely and into a 30px left margin, running bottom-to-top like the spine of the book you came from. It always names the level above — “Compendium” in the list, “Classes” in the detail — so depth is legible without a breadcrumb.

Trade-off Buys back every pixel of vertical space and never competes with the title — but it costs 30px of width on a phone, sits where a swipe-back gesture lives, and vertical type is slower to read. Worth prototyping before committing.

F

Codex bar · sword

B, with the chevron swapped for a shortsword laid point-left — blade, crossguard, grip, pommel. Same row, same target, same centred title; the glyph is the only thing that changes. It nudges a few pixels toward the tip on press.

Trade-off Carries the setting in a spot that is usually generic, and the blade's long axis still reads as “leftward” at a glance. It is a custom react-native-svg shape rather than an Ionicon, and it wants a screenshot at real device scale — a 28×13 sword can silt up on a low-density phone.

Making the blade say “back”

A bare sword names a weapon, not a direction. Four ways to fix that, in increasing order of how much they lean on words: reshape the blade, add the destination, add a caption, or let a chevron lead and keep the sword as flavour. F2 is the only one that is unambiguous on first sight without a learned icon.

F1

Barbed

The point becomes a proper arrowhead with swept barbs, so the silhouette is an arrow first and a sword second. No words added.

Trade-off Keeps F's economy and borrows the one shape everyone already reads as “this way”. The barbs cost the blade its clean taper, so it drifts from sword toward hunting arrow.

F2

Named

Sword plus the place it returns to. The nav line takes over from the eyebrow — no word is repeated — and the title keeps its own line.

Trade-off Nothing to decode: the label says where you land, and the sword is pure flavour beside it. Costs the row of vertical space that plain F saves, and the title is no longer optically centred against a lone glyph.

F3

Captioned

One fixed control at every depth: blade over a small-caps “Back”. The word never changes, so the same component drops in anywhere.

Trade-off Explicit and reusable, and it keeps the title centred. “Back” is generic where the rest of the screen names things — and at 8px the caption is doing a lot of work on a small phone.

F4

Escort

The chevron does the navigating; the sword trails it at half strength as a flourish. Direction is carried by the shape people already know.

Trade-off Safest of the four: it cannot be misread, and the blade still marks the screen as this app's. Two marks in one target is more visual noise than any single glyph, and the sword reads as decoration rather than the control.
